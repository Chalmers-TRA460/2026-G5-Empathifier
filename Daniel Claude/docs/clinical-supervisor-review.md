# Tool Concept: Structured Response Builder for Patient Portal Messages

We are developing a tool to help clinicians deliver higher-quality communication to high-need patients — particularly those with chronic conditions or mental health concerns — when time and cognitive load make this difficult.

We would value your clinical perspective on the concept.

---

## The Problem

Clinicians often know which patients need extra care and warmth in their communication. However, after a demanding day, it can be hard to find the right words. Responses end up informationally correct but emotionally insufficient — the patient receives the facts but does not feel heard or understood. For vulnerable patients, this can erode trust and the therapeutic relationship.

---

## How the Tool Works

The tool is **doctor-triggered and selective** — it is not used for every patient, only for those the clinician identifies as needing extra care.

When the clinician decides to use it, they see a simple structured form instead of a blank text box. The form breaks the response into sections. The clinician fills in only the **clinical facts** in their own words — brief notes, not full sentences. The tool then uses these notes to generate a complete, warm, and empathetic message that the clinician reviews and sends.

**Key principle:** The patient's message is never processed by the AI. The clinician reads it themselves, decides what needs to be addressed, and fills in the form. The AI only processes what the clinician writes.

**Sections in the form:**

| Section | What the clinician fills in |
|--------|----------------------------|
| **Acknowledge** | What did the patient express that needs to be recognized? |
| **Validate** | Why is their concern understandable? (optional) |
| **Concerns** | One block per concern — expandable. Topic, clinical explanation, and what it means for this patient specifically. |
| **Next steps** | What should the patient do? What will the clinician do? |
| **Invite** | A pre-filled closing that the clinician can edit or keep as is. |

Sections left blank are skipped and not included in the generated message.

---

## Example

**Patient message received:**

> "I've been taking the new medication for two weeks but I'm still in pain. I also read online that it can cause liver damage and now I'm really scared. Can I also get a referral to a specialist?"

---

**What the clinician fills into the form:**

- **Acknowledge:** She has been in pain for two weeks and is scared about the medication
- **Validate:** Worrying about side effects when in pain is completely natural
- **Concern 1 — Medication not working:** Takes 4–6 weeks for full effect; she should continue and not stop early
- **Concern 2 — Fear of liver damage:** Very rare, only at high doses over long periods; her current dose is safe
- **Concern 3 — Referral request:** Appropriate after 6 weeks if no improvement; I will initiate the referral now
- **Next steps:** Continue medication. Referral being prepared.
- **Invite:** (default pre-fill accepted)

---

**Message generated by the tool:**

> Hi Anna,
>
> Thank you for your message — I understand it is frustrating to still be in pain, and it is completely natural to worry when you read about possible side effects.
>
> What you read about liver damage is true, but it is important to know that it is very rare and only occurs at high doses over a long period of time. Your current dose is safe and you have nothing to worry about.
>
> Regarding the pain, I want to explain that this medication often takes 4–6 weeks to reach its full effect. It is therefore important that you continue taking it as planned and do not stop the treatment early.
>
> As for your referral — I agree that it is the right way forward if the pain persists. I will initiate the referral now so that it is ready after six weeks if you do not notice any improvement before then.
>
> Please do not hesitate to get in touch if anything changes or if you have any more questions.
>
> Kind regards,
> Dr. [Name]

## Questions for Your Review

We would appreciate your thoughts on the following:

1. **Does the form feel like the right amount of work** for a busy clinician? Too much, too little?
2. **Are the sections the right ones?** Is anything missing or unnecessary from a clinical communication perspective?
3. **Does the generated message strike the right tone?** Is it appropriately warm without feeling generic or patronizing?
4. **Which patient groups do you think would benefit most** from this kind of structured response?
5. **Are there clinical or ethical concerns** we should be aware of before developing this further?

We are at an early concept stage and your feedback will directly shape the direction of the tool. Any thoughts — positive or critical — are very welcome.
